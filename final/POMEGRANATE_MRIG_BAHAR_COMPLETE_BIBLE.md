## 1.1 — Why Soil Comes First (Extra Important Here)

```
At pH 8.2-9.0 (black soil + beluku), organic matter is your #1 tool:
→ Slowly buffers the high pH over years
→ Feeds microbes (VAM, PSB, Azotobacter) that unlock locked Fe/Zn/P
→ Holds moisture in summer, improves drainage of heavy black soil in monsoon
→ A strong soil = fewer sprays needed + disease-resistant trees
Build the soil first. Everything else works better on top of it.
```

## 1.2 — FYM (Farm Yard Manure): Bulk Base

### Preparation
```
Well-rotted cattle dung + bedding (straw/leaves/residue):
1. Layer dung + bedding alternately in heap/pit
2. Keep MOIST (squeezed-sponge), never soggy
3. Turn every 3-4 weeks
4. Ready in 3-4 months: dark, crumbly, earthy smell, COOL to touch
```

### Quantity for 2000 Trees
```
Per tree per YEAR:         35 kg well-rotted FYM
For 2000 trees per YEAR:   35 × 2000 = 70 TONNES

Mrig is ONE main bahar/year, so apply the full dose before it:
→ 35 kg per tree at pre-bahar (May-June) = 70 tonnes for 2000 trees
→ OR split: 20kg pre-bahar + 15kg at post-harvest recovery

RAW MATERIAL (loses ~50% while rotting):
→ ~130-140 tonnes raw dung+bedding/year → buy rotted FYM from dairies if short
```

## 1.5 — SUMMER MANURE (Explained)

```
DOUBT: "Don't put manure in summer (heats soil)?" — depends on TYPE:

❌ RAW/fresh manure in summer = decomposes → generates heat → burns roots. BAD.
✅ FULLY ROTTED compost/FYM = stable, no heat → cools soil, holds moisture. GOOD.

Your Sanjivani (40 days) + FYM (3-4 months) are fully rotted → SAFE in summer.
Test: COOL to touch, crumbly, earthy = safe.

MRIG TIMING (perfect anyway):
→ Apply main manure in May-June (before stress-break) — SW monsoon cools soil.
→ Avoid large fresh applications in peak April dry heat.
```

---

## 1.6 — HOW TO APPLY MANURE

```
WHERE: at CANOPY DRIP-LINE (not trunk — feeder roots are at drip-line).
HOW:
1. Clear mulch aside in a band at drip-line
2. Spread FYM + Sanjivani
3. Lay ON TOP + light shallow mix (NEVER deep-dig — cuts feeder roots)
4. DRENCH Jeevamrutha 10% over the band (activates microbes)
5. Cover with 6-inch MULCH
6. Irrigate to settle

BEST (least root damage): top + Jeevamrutha drench + mulch + irrigate.
```

---

## 1.7 — MULCHING (Critical in Pulivendula)

```
WHY: cools roots in April-May heat, holds moisture, resists monsoon waterlogging
     (improves black-soil structure), builds humus (buffers pH), feeds soil life.
MATERIAL: paddy straw, sugarcane trash, dry leaves, groundnut shells.
THICKNESS: 6 inches at drip-line, 15cm gap from trunk. Neem leaves = pest bonus.
```

# PART 3 — STRESS & FLOWERING (Natural Heat Stress)
# ═══════════════════════════════════════════

## 3.1 — Mrig Stress Is Natural (Easy)

```
March-May in Pulivendula = hot + dry = NATURAL stress. No effort needed.
→ Stop irrigation March. Heat does the work. Tree defoliates + goes dormant.
→ Prune during April (before peak heat): dead wood, water shoots, skirt, center-open.
→ TRCH paste on all cuts (gummosis/wilt entry prevention).
```

## 3.2 — Flowering Protocol (Stress Break, June)

```
STEP 1 — Apply manure (May-June): FYM 35kg + Sanjivani 5kg/tree + Jeevamrutha + mulch
STEP 2 — GIVE WATER (break stress, June): start drip
STEP 3 — FLOWERING SPRAY (Day 1 + Day 10):
   🌿 MKP (00:52:34) 5g/L + Zinc 5g/L + Borax 2g/L → canopy
   = High P+K+Zn+B, ZERO Nitrogen → triggers flowers
STEP 4 — DRIP: Jeevamrutha/EJEV + MOE + VAM. NO nitrogen till set.

⚠️ NEVER UREA at flowering (makes shoots, not flowers).
⚠️ SW monsoon may bring rain at flowering → Copper preventive (see P2).
```
