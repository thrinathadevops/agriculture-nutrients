## A1 — FYM (Farm Yard Manure) — The Bulk Base

### What FYM Is
```
FYM = well-rotted cattle dung + urine-soaked bedding (straw/crop residue/dry leaves)
It is the BULK organic matter — builds soil structure, holds water,
feeds earthworms and microbes, buffers your pH 8.2 over time.
```

### FYM Preparation (Simple)
```
1. Collect cattle dung + bedding (straw, dry leaves, crop residue)
2. Make a heap or pit — layer dung and bedding alternately
3. Sprinkle water — keep MOIST (like squeezed sponge), never soggy
4. Turn the heap every 3-4 weeks (air helps decomposition)
5. Cover with a thin soil/straw layer (reduces loss)
6. Ready in 3-4 months when:
   → Dark brown, crumbly
   → Earthy smell (NO dung smell)
   → Original straw/dung not recognizable
   → Cool to touch (decomposition finished — IMPORTANT for summer)
```

### FYM Quantity for 1000 Trees
```
Per tree per year: 35 kg well-rotted FYM
For 1000 trees: 35 kg × 1000 = 35 TONNES per year

SPLIT into two applications (before each bahar):
→ 17-18 kg per tree before each bahar
→ ~17.5 tonnes per application for 1000 trees

RAW MATERIAL NEEDED (FYM loses ~50% mass while rotting):
→ You need ~65-70 tonnes of raw dung + bedding per year to get 35 tonnes FYM
→ One cow gives ~5-8 tonnes dung/year → you need dung from 8-10 cattle
→ OR buy well-rotted FYM from local dairies (common in your area)
```

## A4 — THE SUMMER MANURE QUESTION (Your Doubt — Explained)

```
YOUR CONFUSION: "People say don't put manure in summer — it heats the soil.
But you told me to give it. Which is correct?"

BOTH ARE CORRECT — because it depends on the TYPE of manure:

❌ RAW / FRESH / HALF-ROTTED manure in summer = BAD
   → It is still decomposing → decomposition GENERATES HEAT
   → In hot soil, this extra heat + ammonia release can BURN feeder roots
   → It also draws moisture and stresses roots in dry heat
   → THIS is what the old farmers correctly warn against

✅ FULLY DECOMPOSED / WELL-ROTTED compost + FYM in summer = GOOD
   → It is STABLE — decomposition already finished → NO heat generation
   → It is cool, dark, crumbly (that "cool to touch" test matters)
   → It actually COOLS the soil (acts as mulch, holds moisture)
   → It feeds soil life during stressful heat

THE RESOLUTION:
→ The warning is about RAW manure. Your Sanjivani (40 days) and FYM (3-4 months)
  are FULLY DECOMPOSED → safe and beneficial even in summer.
→ NEVER apply fresh dung directly in summer.
→ ALWAYS check: cool, crumbly, earthy smell = safe to apply.

BEST TIMING ANYWAY (to be safest):
→ Apply the MAIN manure dose at the START of each bahar cycle
  (when you break stress and give water — soil is being moistened anyway)
→ For the July (Mrig) bahar: apply in early July (monsoon cools things)
→ For the January (Ambia) bahar: apply late December (winter, cool — perfect)
→ AVOID the peak dry heat of April-May for large fresh applications
→ If you must feed in peak summer, use only well-rotted + apply under mulch + water in
```

## A5 — HOW TO APPLY MANURE (Method — Your Question)

```
WHERE TO APPLY (very important for citrus):
→ NOT at the trunk (feeder roots are NOT there + trunk rot risk)
→ Apply in a BAND / RING at the CANOPY DRIP-LINE
  (the drip-line = the circle under the outer edge of the branches)
→ This is exactly where the feeder roots are actively feeding

HOW TO APPLY (step by step):
1. Make a shallow ring — either a light trench (10-15cm deep) at drip-line,
   OR just clear the mulch aside in a band at drip-line
2. Spread the FYM + Sanjivani compost in the band
3. LIGHTLY mix into the top 10-15cm ONLY
   → Shallow — do NOT dig deep (deep tillage cuts feeder roots)
   → OR simply lay it on top and cover with mulch (no digging — even safer)
4. DRENCH with diluted Jeevamrutha over the manure
   → This activates the microbes, speeds nutrient release
   → Jeevamrutha 10% (20L Jeevamrutha in 200L water) poured over the band
5. Cover with MULCH (straw / dry leaves / sugarcane trash) 6 inches thick
   → Mulch prevents heat, holds moisture, slowly feeds soil
6. IRRIGATE to settle it in

TOP vs INCORPORATE — the answer:
→ BOTH work. For safety and least root damage:
   BEST = lay on top + drench Jeevamrutha + cover with mulch + irrigate
   (no digging = no root damage, mulch protects from heat)
→ Light incorporation (shallow mix) is fine too, speeds availability
→ NEVER deep-dig manure into citrus root zone (damages feeder roots)

FREQUENCY:
→ Main dose: before each bahar (2× per year)
→ Jeevamrutha drench: continues through the drip calendar
→ Mulch: maintain 6-inch layer year-round (top up as it decomposes)
```

# PART C — STRESS & FLOWERING (Your January Scenario Explained)

## Controlled Stress (Drip-Based) — Why It's BETTER Than Rain

```
YOU CLARIFIED: In January (Ambia bahar), no rain — you control water by drip.
→ To STRESS: you STOP the drip → soil dries → tree goes dormant
→ To RELIEVE: you START the drip → tree wakes → flowers

THIS IS ACTUALLY THE BETTER BAHAR:
→ You control everything → no rain to break the stress
→ Uniform stress → UNIFORM flowering (the problem you had in July)
→ Predictable timing → better planning
→ The January (Ambia) bahar should give you your BEST, most even flowering
→ The July (Mrig) bahar is the risky one (rain breaks stress → uneven flowering)
```

## The Flowering-Boost Protocol (During Controlled Stress Relief)

```
STEP 1 — COMPLETE THE STRESS (15-20 days no drip):
→ Leaves show mild wilting / slight yellowing = correct stress
→ Do NOT give water yet. Do NOT spray. Tree is resting.

STEP 2 — APPLY MANURE (before giving water):
→ FYM 17 kg + Sanjivani 5 kg per tree at drip-line + mulch
→ (This is the pre-bahar main manure dose)

STEP 3 — GIVE WATER (break stress) + FLOWERING BOOST:
→ Start drip — light irrigation first, then normal
→ FLOWERING BOOST SPRAY (this is the key — replaces your urea mistake):
   🌿 SPRAY: 00:52:34 (MKP) 5g/L + Zinc Sulfate 5g/L + Borax 2g/L
   → This is HIGH Phosphorus + Potassium + Zinc + Boron, ZERO Nitrogen
   → It TRIGGERS flowering (not shoots)
   → 5kg MKP + 5kg Zinc + 2kg Borax in 1000L → spray whole canopy
→ Repeat this spray after 10 days (Day 1 and Day 10)

STEP 4 — DRIP (basal flowering nutrition):
→ Jeevamrutha/EJEV + MOE (K + pH) + VAM
→ NO nitrogen (no urea, no high-N) until fruit sets

⚠️ NEVER APPLY UREA / NITROGEN AT FLOWERING
   → This is what gave you shoots instead of flowers last time
   → Nitrogen = leaves. MKP (P+K) = flowers. Remember this forever.
```

## Flowering Troubleshooting — When Buds/Flush Don't Come

```
COMMON PROBLEM: pruned → gave water → rain fell in middle → shoots not buds
(or no flush). This is a known citrus issue. Principle: MORE STRESS = MORE FLOWERS.
Rain removes the stress, so flowering fails.

SAFE METHOD (always FIRST):
1. Wait for soil to dry back to mild stress (dull leaves, no new weeds)
   — do NOT spray MKP onto wet/rain-soaked soil (weak result).
2. On a stressed tree, spray (MIX, one tank, per 1000L):
   00:52:34 (MKP) 5 kg + 13:00:45 (KNO3) 5 kg + Boron 2 kg
   (high P + K, ZERO nitrogen). Optional: Wettable Sulphur 2-2.5 g/L (mild fungicide).
3. Repeat after 10-15 days. Buds should appear. NO urea anywhere near flowering.

PACLOBUTRAZOL — LAST RESORT ONLY (⚠️ strong PGR):
Paclobutrazol 23% SC @ 1-2 ml/L forces flowering by stopping shoot growth.
⚠️ Only if stress + MKP repeatedly fails AND trees are MATURE (8-year).
⚠️ NEVER on young trees. Long soil residual (1-2 yr). Overdose = permanent
   stunting (start LOW, 1 ml/L). Some export markets restrict its residue.
⚠️ Consult local horticulture officer first.
RULE: Master natural stress + MKP first. Paclobutrazol is a cautious
      last-resort for mature trees only — never first choice.
```

⭐ THE ONE MUST-DO: MKP (00:52:34) flowering spray — NO urea
💬 WHY: MKP triggers flowers. Zinc prevents little-leaf. Boron readies pollen.

## PERIOD 2 (Days 16-30) — FULL BLOOM
```
🌿 SPRAY (evening, Day 16): Borax 2g/L + Zinc 5g/L → canopy (NOT on open flowers)
🌿 SPRAY 2 (Calcium, SEPARATE): skip on open flowers — do via drip instead
💧 DRIP Day 21: Calcium Nitrate 5g/L + MOE 2L (Ca for pollen tube — SEPARATE from P)
💧 DRIP Day 24: EJEV 30L + MOE 2L
💧 DRIP Day 30: Trichoderma 10L + MOE 2L

⭐ THE ONE MUST-DO: Boron spray (pollen tube = fruit set)
💬 WHY: No Boron = pollen fails = no fruit. Calcium via DRIP (not spray) because
        Ca must not mix with phosphorus. Evening spray only (protect pollen).
🦠 IF RAIN: Copper Oxychloride 3g/L (canker) — separate spray, different day
```

## PERIOD 3 (Days 31-45) — FRUIT SET (Reduce Drop)
```
🌿 SPRAY 1 (morning): EJEV 100L + NAA (Planofix) 1.5ml/4.5L → canopy
   (NAA the anti-drop hormone mixes with EJEV — this is ONE spray)
🌿 SPRAY 2 (evening, SEPARATE): Calcium — BTM 30L OR Calcium Nitrate spray on fruitlets
💧 DRIP Day 33: VAM 10L + MOE 2L
💧 DRIP Day 39: Calcium Nitrate 5g/L + MOE 2L (SEPARATE from P — reduces June drop)
💧 DRIP Day 42: EJEV 30L + Coconut water 10L (Zeatin reduces drop) + MOE 2L

⭐ THE ONE MUST-DO: NAA (morning) + Calcium (evening) — TWO SEPARATE sprays
💬 WHY: Citrus drops 70-80% of fruitlets. NAA (auxin) holds them. Calcium strengthens
        the stalk. These are TWO sprays (NAA morning with EJEV, Calcium evening alone).
        Still NO nitrogen (N causes more drop until fruit firmly set).
```

## PERIOD 4 (Days 46-60) — EARLY CELL DIVISION (SIZE CEILING)
```
🌿 SPRAY 1 (morning): GA3 15ppm + Zinc 5g/L → canopy (SIZE BOOST)
🌿 SPRAY 2 (if pests): Leaf-miner control — Imidacloprid 0.3ml/L or Spinosad
💧 DRIP: NOW nitrogen begins — 19:19:19 5g/L OR EJEV+NPKD (fruit is set, N helps)
💧 DRIP Day 51: Calcium Nitrate 5g/L (SEPARATE) + MOE 2L
💧 DRIP: EJEV 30L + Jeevamrutha + MOE 2L

⭐ THE ONE MUST-DO: GA3 + Zinc (size) — and START nitrogen now
💬 WHY: Cell division decides MAX fruit size (cannot fix later). GA3 = more cells.
        Nitrogen is NOW correct (fruit set) — it builds cells. This is when N helps,
        unlike at flowering when it hurt you. Protect leaves (leaf miner) = sugar factory.
```

## PERIOD 5-6 (Days 61-90) — ACTIVE DIVISION → TRANSITION + THIN
```
🌿 SPRAY: 19:19:19 5g/L + Zinc + Iron (Fe-EDDHA 1g/L if leaves yellow)
💧 DRIP: EJEV + NPKD (N + P) + MOE 2L; Calcium Nitrate separate session
💧 DRIP: Start Potassium — SOP 00:00:50 5g/L from Period 6

⭐ THE ONE MUST-DO (Period 6): THIN THE FRUIT + start Potassium
💬 WHY: After June drop, remove small/damaged/clustered fruit → remaining fruit
        get BIGGER. Critical for two-bahar (never overload). Iron (Fe-EDDHA) fixes
        yellow leaves — only EDDHA works at pH 8.2, ordinary iron fails.
```

## PERIOD 7-9 (Days 91-135) — CELL ENLARGEMENT (SIZE + QUALITY)
```
🌿 SPRAY: SOP 00:00:50 5g/L (Potassium — drives size + Brix) + Iron if yellow
🌿 SPRAY 2 (SEPARATE): Calcium Nitrate 5g/L OR BTM on fruit (peel + no cracking)
💧 DRIP: MOE 3L (high K) + reduce nitrogen + Jeevamrutha + MOE for pH
💧 DRIP: Trichoderma maintenance

⭐ THE ONE MUST-DO: Potassium (SOP) — the size + sweetness nutrient
💬 WHY: K drives water into cells = SIZE. K also = Brix (sugar) + juice = QUALITY.
        Calcium (separate) = firm peel, no cracking, no granulation. Keep water STEADY
        (fluctuation = cracking + drop). Reduce nitrogen through here.
```

## PERIOD 10-12 (Days 136-180) — MATURATION (QUALITY FINISH)
```
🌿 SPRAY: SOP 00:00:50 5g/L (final Brix + color)
🌿 SPRAY 2 (SEPARATE): Calcium — shelf life + no granulation
💧 DRIP: MOE 4L (max K) + Jeevamrutha + MOE for pH — NO NITROGEN
💧 Last 2-3 weeks: mild water deficit (concentrates sugar = higher Brix)

⭐ THE ONE MUST-DO: High Potassium + ZERO Nitrogen
💬 WHY: K = maximum sweetness + juice + peel color. Nitrogen NOW ruins Brix, keeps
        acid high, delays color. Stop ALL chemicals 30 days before harvest (clean fruit).
        Mild late water deficit boosts Brix. Cool nights help color naturally.
🦠 Canker on fruit: Copper (only if needed + observe 30-day pre-harvest stop)
```

💬 WHY: Next bahar's FLOWER QUALITY depends on reserves rebuilt NOW. Skip this =
        weak flowers + poor set next cycle. This is the secret to sustainable two-bahar.
