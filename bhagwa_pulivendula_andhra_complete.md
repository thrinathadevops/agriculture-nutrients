## Beluku (White Beluku) — What It Is and What It Does

```
"BELUKU" in AP/Rayalaseema farming context:

Most likely options:
1. CHUNNAM / LIME (Calcium Hydroxide Ca(OH)2):
   → White powder, highly alkaline
   → pH effect: RAISES soil pH significantly
   → 1 ton per acre raises pH by 0.5-1.0 units

2. DOLAMITE (Calcium Magnesium Carbonate CaMg(CO3)2):
   → White granular material
   → Adds Ca + Mg → pH raises moderately

3. GYPSUM (Calcium Sulfate CaSO4):
   → White granular, slightly soluble
   → BEST FOR BLACK SOIL → improves structure + drainage
   → Does NOT raise pH significantly (slight lowering)
   → Adds Ca + Sulfur

MOST CRITICAL ASSESSMENT:
If your beluku = Lime (Chunnam):
→ Your soil pH is NOW approximately 8.5–9.0+
→ Water pH 8.2 + Black soil 7.8 + Lime application = 8.5–9.0

At pH 8.5–9.0:
→ Iron (Fe): COMPLETELY locked — tree shows severe yellowing
→ Zinc (Zn): COMPLETELY locked — small leaf, poor fruit size
→ Manganese (Mn): Locked — pale leaves, poor photosynthesis
→ Boron (B): Partially available but reduced
→ Phosphorus: 80% locked in soil as calcium phosphate
→ ONLY Ca and Mg: Abundantly available (you have too much of these)

This is why you MUST use:
1. TNAM (Chinta Neerru) — tamarind water as natural acidifier
2. Nano Urea + Nano DAP — spray bypasses soil pH completely
3. FAA spray — chelated Fe bypasses soil
4. ALV in drip — natural acid + chelator
5. PKA (Azotobacter siderophores) — Fe siderophores work even at pH 9.0

FUTURE RECOMMENDATION:
If you must apply white material to soil:
→ GYPSUM (calcium sulfate) is MUCH BETTER for black soil than lime
→ Gypsum improves drainage, adds Ca + S, does NOT raise pH
→ Talk to your local agriculture department about gypsum subsidy in AP
→ Stop lime application — your soil has excess Ca already from black soil + beluku
```

## Why Chinta Neerru is Your Most Important Local Input

```
PULIVENDULA-SPECIFIC PROBLEM:
Your soil pH = 8.5–9.0 (black soil + beluku + hard water)

Standard inputs (MOE, BTM) have pH 3.5–4.5 — good acidifiers
BUT in 100L header tank with 8.2 pH hard water:
→ MOE 1L in 99L water = barely changes pH
→ Need stronger, more effective natural acid

CHINTA (TAMARIND) NEERRU:
→ Tartaric acid: pH 2.9 (stronger than citric acid)
→ Malic acid: additional acidification
→ Tartaric acid is a CHELATING AGENT
→ Chelation means: tartaric acid GRABS locked Fe, Zn, Mn
→ Forms tartrate complexes → plant absorbs directly
→ This is the same chelation principle as EDDHA (chemical chelate)
→ BUT completely natural — traditional AP farming wisdom

RAYALASEEMA ADVANTAGE:
→ Tamarind trees are abundant in Pulivendula region
→ Old tamarind = cheapest, most available natural input
→ Farmers already know this plant — no new learning needed
→ Cost = nearly zero (tamarind waste from household use)
```

---

## Chinta Neerru Preparation

```
Method 1 — Quick (24 hours):
→ Take 2 kg fresh tamarind (with seed pod — no need to remove seeds)
→ Soak in 20 liters of water
→ Soak for 24 hours — squeeze and massage pods during soaking
→ Strain through cloth — remove fibres and seeds
→ You have 15-18L of Chinta Neerru (mother concentrate)
→ Use within 5 days — keep in shade

Method 2 — Fermented (3–5 days):
→ Same ingredients but ferment 3–5 days
→ Slight fermentation increases organic acid concentration
→ Also develops LAB (lactic acid bacteria) — additional disease control
→ Ready: Sour smell + slight brown foam
→ More potent than Method 1 — preferred for drip

pH of mother concentrate:
→ Method 1: pH 2.8–3.2
→ Method 2: pH 2.5–3.0
→ When diluted 2% in 100L tank: brings header tank to pH 5.5–6.5 ✅
```

# SECTION 5 — PULIVENDULA SPECIFIC CHALLENGES

## Challenge 1 — Extreme Heat (April-May: 40-44°C)

```
IMPACT ON POMEGRANATE:
→ Fruit surface temperature can reach 55-60°C = sunburn = brown patches on peel
→ Flower drop if late Ambe Bahar flowers exposed to 40°C+
→ Root zone temperature in black soil rises to 38°C = root damage
→ Rapid water loss through leaves = wilting
→ Fruit cracking increases with heat-induced uneven water movement

PROTECTION PROTOCOL:
→ KLN (Kaolin clay): WHITE COATING on fruit = reflects 30% of solar radiation
   Critical for Pulivendula — double the dose: 500g per 100L from P4
→ CNW spray: Coconut water + cooling effect whenever temp > 42°C
→ SAG spray: Mannitol + Betaines = internal heat protection
→ Irrigation: ONLY early morning (4-6 AM) and evening (6-8 PM) during April-May
→ Mulching: 6-inch thick neem leaf or paddy straw mulch under each tree
   → Reduces soil temperature by 8-10°C
   → Prevents extreme crack formation
   → Slow-releases Azadirachtin from neem mulch into soil
→ Shade net: 25% shade net on south-facing side during May (optional but helpful)
```

## Challenge 2 — Black Soil Deep Cracking (November-January)

```
WHAT HAPPENS:
→ Black cotton soil shrinks on drying → cracks 2-5cm wide, 30-60cm deep
→ Cracks cut through feeding roots → root damage
→ Cracks break VAM mycorrhizal network built through season
→ Air pockets form around roots → root desiccation
→ Cracks expose deeper soil → nematodes redistribute

MANAGEMENT:
→ MULCHING (most important solution):
   6-inch paddy straw or dry neem leaves in 1-meter circle around each tree
   Apply before stress period begins (September for Ambe Bahar batch)
   Reduces cracking by 60-70% by maintaining soil moisture

→ GRADUAL STRESS: Do not stop irrigation abruptly
   Taper irrigation over 2 weeks before stress period
   Tree adjusts root system gradually — less crack damage

→ TRCH after cracking season:
   First irrigation of new season → TRCH 5% drip immediately
   Rebuilds fungal network broken by cracks

→ VAM reapplication every season is essential (not once for life)
   Cracking destroys previous network → rebuild every Ambe Bahar start
```

## Challenge 3 — NE Monsoon Waterlogging (October-December)

```
CRITICAL RISK: Black soil + NE monsoon rainfall = waterlogging
Waterlogged black soil = perfect condition for:
→ Fusarium wilt to spread
→ Phytophthora root rot
→ Bacterial blight explosive spread
→ Nematode redistribution

STRUCTURAL SOLUTIONS (do before planting or during next season):
→ BUNDING: Raise planting mounds 30-45cm above ground level
   Plant trees on these raised mounds — roots stay above waterline
→ DRAINAGE CHANNELS: Between rows — every 14 feet row spacing = perfect for channels
   Dig 30cm wide, 45cm deep channels between rows
→ BROAD BED FURROW (BBF): Convert to BBF system for better water management

INPUT SOLUTIONS:
→ PLF (Pseudomonas): Produces antibiotics that work even in waterlogged soil
   Some Pseudomonas strains fix N even in low-oxygen conditions
→ TRCH: Trichoderma tolerates temporary waterlogging
→ TNAM (Chinta Neerru): Apply before expected rain → improves clay structure
→ ALV + TRM spray: Before and after every rain event — prevents blight outbreak
→ PLF + TRCH drip: Immediately after water recedes from field
```
